The framework

Six domains of student wellbeing

Second Bell looks at student wellbeing across six connected domains. A difficulty in one area, such as poor sleep, often affects another, such as emotional balance or attention. Looking at them together helps schools move from reacting to problems towards supporting students earlier.

  • Physical Readiness
  • Attention & Effort
  • Emotional Balance
  • Digital & Lifestyle
  • Adolescent Health
  • Sports Safety
Domain 01

Physical Readiness

Posture, core strength, stamina, and whether a student can sit, focus, and take part without pain or discomfort.

Why it mattersPoor posture can limit how deeply a student breathes, which can bring on tiredness by mid-morning.

Domain 02

Attention & Effort

How much focus a student has left after physical strain, too little sleep, or sensory overload.

Why it mattersOngoing tiredness can make it much harder for a child to keep their attention on a task.

Domain 03

Emotional Balance

Signs of stress, anxiety, or difficulty managing emotions that can show up as behaviour in the classroom.

Why it mattersStress that goes unrecognised is often mistaken for defiance.

Domain 04

Digital & Lifestyle

How screen time, disrupted sleep, and inactive habits affect a student's performance at school.

Why it mattersUsing devices late at night can reduce REM sleep, the stage of sleep that helps the brain store memories.

Domain 05

Adolescent Health

Awareness and support for students going through the physical and emotional changes of puberty.

Why it mattersHormonal changes call for structured, caring guidance rather than discipline alone.

Domain 06

Sports Safety

Biomechanical screening, which checks how the body moves, to help prevent repeat injuries in active students.

Why it mattersSpotting movement problems early can help prevent long-term joint pain.

In the classroom

Everyday signals teachers may notice

These are the signs that can show up during a normal school day. They are a separate list from the six domains above.

  • Attention & Readiness

    Difficulty focusing or understanding lessons because of tiredness or mental overload.

  • Emotional Regulation

    Trouble managing stress, frustration, or relationships with classmates.

  • Student Fatigue

    Ongoing tiredness that affects participation, memory, and physical activity.

  • Digital Habits

    A lot of screen time that affects sleep and leads to long inactive hours.

  • Posture & Physicality

    Poor sitting habits that lead to aches, pain, and less stamina through the day.

  • Student Wellbeing

    The overall picture of a child's safety, mental health, and physical balance.

An observation is not a diagnosis. These signals are a reason to look more closely. Assessment and diagnosis must come from a qualified healthcare professional.
