About the programme

What Is Second Bell?

Second Bell is a school health and wellbeing programme by RECOUP Health. It connects what teachers notice in class, health screening on campus, and the right support, so schools can better understand the issues that may affect students during the school day.

  • Understand student health and wellbeing

    See how posture, sleep, stress, screen habits, and physical readiness show up in the school day.

  • Identify concerns that may affect learning

    Non-invasive screening on campus helps flag concerns that can affect learning and participation early.

  • Connect students with the right support

    When a need is found, the school receives guidance on next steps, including specialist support where appropriate.

Why it matters

What Happens Beyond the Report Card?

A report card shows results. It does not always show what is behind them. Tiredness, posture, stress, or poor sleep can affect a student long before marks change, and each person around the student sees only part of the picture.

At school

Teachers notice the signs

Teachers may notice fatigue, changes in attention, posture, or behaviour. They are often the first to see something, but they are not expected to diagnose the cause.

At home

Parents see daily changes

Parents may notice changes in sleep, mood, energy, or daily habits. Often they only learn about a problem at school through a report, months later.

Through health assessment

Professionals assess the cause

Qualified professionals can assess concerns that need further attention and recommend what should happen next.

Second Bell brings these perspectives together.

Instead of three separate views, the school gets one clearer picture, with a structured route from a classroom observation to proper assessment and support. School leadership makes the decisions at every step.

Please note: a classroom observation is not a medical diagnosis. It is a reason to look more closely, with the help of a qualified professional.

The framework

Six Domains. A Broader View of Student Wellbeing.

Explore the health and wellbeing areas that help schools understand the many factors that can affect a student's school day. The domains are connected: a difficulty in one, such as poor sleep, often affects another.

Domain 01 of 06

Physical Readiness

Posture, core strength, stamina, and whether a student can sit, focus, and take part without pain or discomfort.

Why it mattersPoor posture can limit how deeply a student breathes, which can bring on tiredness by mid-morning.

Read more about this domain
Domain 02 of 06

Attention & Effort

How much focus a student has left after physical strain, too little sleep, or sensory overload.

Why it mattersOngoing tiredness can make it much harder for a child to keep their attention on a task.

Read more about this domain
Domain 03 of 06

Emotional Balance

Signs of stress, anxiety, or difficulty managing emotions that can show up as behaviour in the classroom.

Why it mattersStress that goes unrecognised is often mistaken for defiance.

Read more about this domain
Domain 04 of 06

Digital & Lifestyle

How screen time, disrupted sleep, and inactive habits affect a student's performance at school.

Why it mattersUsing devices late at night can reduce REM sleep, the stage of sleep that helps the brain store memories.

Read more about this domain
Domain 05 of 06

Adolescent Health

Awareness and support for students going through the physical and emotional changes of puberty.

Why it mattersHormonal changes call for structured, caring guidance rather than discipline alone.

Read more about this domain
Domain 06 of 06

Sports Safety

Biomechanical screening, which checks how the body moves, to help prevent repeat injuries in active students.

Why it mattersSpotting movement problems early can help prevent long-term joint pain.

The programme

What Does Second Bell Include?

Four connected parts, delivered on campus and planned around the school week.

Preventive health screening

Non-invasive checks carried out on campus, so small issues can be noticed before they affect learning.

  • EPHR mapping
  • Postural and biomechanical checks
  • Vision and basic hearing checks

Health education workshops

Practical sessions for students, teachers, and parents, so healthy habits continue beyond the school gate.

  • Ergonomics and posture
  • Nutrition for brain development
  • Navigating puberty

Teacher training

Helps teachers recognise possible concerns, such as telling a distracted student from a tired one, without expecting them to diagnose.

  • Speech and motor delays
  • Behavioural health first aid

Specialist support when appropriate

A clear route to further assessment when screening shows that a student may need more help.

  • Child psychologists

How it works

Getting Started Is Simple.

The programme runs in three phases, and the school stays in charge of each one.

  1. 1Phase 1

    Step 1: Initial discussion

    A conversation with school leadership about your school's culture, priorities, timetable, and the health measures already in place.

  2. 2Phase 2

    Step 2: Baseline assessment

    Structured, non-invasive screening on campus, planned around classes. It covers posture, biomechanics, developmental markers, and readiness to learn.

  3. 3Phase 2

    Step 4: Plan the next steps

  • Minimal administrative burden
  • School leadership stays in control

RECOUP School Health Score

Understand Your School's Health and Wellbeing Picture.

The RECOUP School Health Score (RSHS) brings screening observations together into one score out of 100. Individual results are read together, so leadership can see campus-wide trends and decide where support is most needed.

What leadership receives

  • Campus insights

    An overview of health and wellbeing trends across the school.

  • Domain-by-domain analysis

    How the school is doing across the six domains of the framework.

  • Early risk identification

    Possible physical or emotional barriers to learning, flagged before they grow.

  • Practical recommendations

    Clear, structured next steps written for school leadership.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Can't find your answer? Talk to the RECOUP Health team.

What is Second Bell?

Second Bell is a school health and wellbeing programme by RECOUP Health. It combines preventive screening on campus, health education workshops, teacher training, and specialist support, so schools can better understand health issues that may affect learning.

Who can enrol a school?

Enrolment is started by school leadership: owners, management, or the principal and leadership team. Suitability is discussed in a first consultation.

Parents and students cannot enrol a school on their own, but they are welcome to share Second Bell with their school's leadership.

What does the framework cover?

Second Bell looks at student wellbeing across six connected domains: physical readiness, attention and effort, emotional balance, digital and lifestyle habits, adolescent health, and sports safety. A difficulty in one area, such as poor sleep, often affects another. Explore the framework.

Will the programme disrupt regular classes?

It is designed not to. Activities are planned around your existing timetable in the first consultation, with minimal administrative work for staff. The school stays in control throughout.

What does preventive screening involve?

Non-invasive checks carried out on campus. These include EPHR mapping, postural and biomechanical checks, and vision and basic hearing checks.

What happens when a concern is identified?

The school receives recommendations for suitable next steps. Where appropriate, a student can be directed to further assessment or support, for example from paediatric specialists, child psychologists, or speech and occupational therapists.

What does the school receive?

After baseline screening, school leadership receives the first RECOUP School Health Score, along with campus-wide insights, a domain-by-domain analysis, early identification of possible risks, and practical recommendations. Read about the score.

Does Second Bell replace a medical consultation?

No. Classroom observations and screening can show that something needs a closer look, but they are not a diagnosis. Diagnosis and treatment must come from an appropriate healthcare professional.
